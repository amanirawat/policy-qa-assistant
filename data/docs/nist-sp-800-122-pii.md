# Protecting Personally Identifiable Information (NIST SP 800-122)

## What is personally identifiable information
Personally identifiable information (PII) is any information that can be used to distinguish or trace a person's identity, such as name, Social Security number, date and place of birth, or biometric records. It also includes information that is linked or linkable to a person, such as medical, educational, financial, and employment information.

## PII confidentiality impact levels
Organizations should assign each set of PII a confidentiality impact level of low, moderate, or high, based on the harm that would result if it were disclosed. Factors include how easily the data identifies a person, how many people's records are involved, how sensitive each data field is, the context in which the data is used, legal obligations, and where and how the data is accessed and stored.

## Minimizing the collection and retention of PII
The best way to protect PII is to collect only what is truly necessary for a specific purpose and to keep it only as long as needed. Organizations should regularly review their PII holdings and securely dispose of data that is no longer required.

## De-identification and anonymization
Where possible, PII should be de-identified by removing or obscuring identifying fields so the remaining data cannot reasonably be linked back to a person. Anonymized data can often be used for analysis and testing instead of real records.

## Security controls for PII
Appropriate safeguards for PII include role-based access controls so only authorized people can see it, encryption of PII when stored and when transmitted, logging and auditing of access, and secure handling of mobile devices and removable media that hold PII.

## Training employees on handling PII
Employees who handle PII should receive training on what counts as PII, how to handle and share it securely, the rules for using it, and how to report a suspected breach.

## Responding to a PII breach
Incident response plans should include procedures specific to breaches involving PII. This includes determining what data was exposed, assessing the potential harm to affected individuals, and deciding whether and how to notify them.
