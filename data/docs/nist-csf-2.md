# NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0

## Purpose of the framework
The NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0, released in February 2024, gives organizations of any size or sector a common language for understanding, assessing, prioritizing, and communicating cybersecurity risk. It describes desired outcomes rather than prescribing specific tools or technologies.

## The six core functions
CSF 2.0 organizes cybersecurity outcomes into six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover.

## Govern
Govern covers the organization's cybersecurity risk management strategy, expectations, and policy. It includes defining roles and responsibilities, setting risk tolerance, overseeing cybersecurity, and managing supply chain risk. Govern was added in version 2.0.

## Identify
Identify means understanding the organization's current cybersecurity risks. This includes keeping an inventory of hardware, software, data, and services, and assessing risks to those assets.

## Protect
Protect covers safeguards that manage risk, including identity management and access control, security awareness training, data security, platform security, and the resilience of technology infrastructure.

## Detect
Detect means finding and analyzing possible cybersecurity attacks and compromises through continuous monitoring and analysis of anomalies and events.

## Respond
Respond covers the actions taken once an incident is detected, including incident management, analysis, reporting and communication, and mitigation.

## Recover
Recover covers restoring assets and operations affected by an incident and communicating during recovery.

## Profiles and tiers
An organizational profile describes an organization's current or target cybersecurity posture in terms of CSF outcomes. Comparing current and target profiles helps identify gaps and prioritize work. Tiers describe how rigorous an organization's risk governance and management practices are, ranging from Tier 1 Partial, to Tier 2 Risk Informed, Tier 3 Repeatable, and Tier 4 Adaptive.
